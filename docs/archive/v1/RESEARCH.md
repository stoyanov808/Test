# Official-source research for СТУДЕНТСКИ ГРАД

Research completed before game implementation. Official source pages and sheets were retrieved on 6 October 2026 over direct HTTPS with certificate verification retained. Local evidence is in `sources/`: original HTML, extracted CMS title-specific feature descriptions, PDF sheets, extracted PDF text, and download receipts containing URLs, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes. These are researched commercial examples; the proposed game is an original virtual-euro demo with independently specified mathematics, assets and audio.

## What was actually inspected

Three official game sheets are used below: Mental, Tombstone R.I.P and Folsom Prison. The current Duck Hunters, Fire in the Hole 3 and Outsourced 2 pages have title-specific feature descriptions and public mathematics tables; their page documents do not link a game-sheet PDF. Their official feature footage supplies additional presentation evidence. Sound observations are limited to available media metadata/amplitude analysis because direct audio listening is unavailable in this environment. Do not represent the original soundtrack plan as an observed Nolimit City soundtrack.

Two source defects matter: Mental’s current long-form CMS summary incorrectly discusses Das xBoot, and Duck Hunters’ summary says 5×5 whereas its mathematics table and actual official footage show six reels with five rows. The mismatched summaries are excluded. Generic xMechanics explanations describe families of modifiers, sometimes differently from a title’s specific rules; title-specific descriptions and sheets take precedence.

Measured audio amplitude supplies a limited sound comparison: Mental’s sample dips during its red-door transition and increases during the count-up; the sampled Folsom Chair sections stay relatively louder and flatter. The sources show an approach worth adapting: a clear scene identity, expressive illustrated symbols, a large reel area, readable modifier causes before the resulting payoff, visible persisted progress, and distinct presentations for stronger features. No single universal reel-stop timing or soundtrack was established. Original normal/turbo timings should be chosen and tested for readability on phones.

## Adaptation to Studentski Grad

The new game keeps the user’s five reels and four rows: 1,024 initial ways, adjacent matching reels from the left, minimum three reels. It does not adopt Duck Hunters’ eight-symbol scatter pays or Fire in the Hole’s expanding six-reel model. Lower pays become textbooks, coffee, instant noodles and doner; premiums become illustrated adult university students, a DJ and a party couple.

Mental’s frame accumulation suggests colorful sticky lecture-note borders that visibly split a paying symbol into two copies. Tombstone’s Wild motion suggests a comic adult friend stumbling into a full reel, with a separate +1 multiplier cue per movement. The brief’s bonus party meter applies its current multiplier first and increments after a win, making progress readable. Precise original Wild combination and split rules are documented in [the engine design](MATH-DESIGN.md), rather than borrowed from Outsourced 2’s additive global Wild rule.

The three bonus scenes increase both mechanics and atmosphere: a dorm room with textbooks, drinks and fairy lights; a busy Studentski street with club lights and doner shops; and an 8 December stage with lights and confetti. Accumulated frames, Wild progress and party energy survive upgrades. The drama comes from perceivable retained state, while a finite retrigger limit keeps the entire paid round bounded.

Boosters and buys get separate illustrated tabs with exact euro costs and confirmation before debit. Base bet and actual spin price remain separate; buys never stack with booster charges. The feature artwork and short event messages use Bulgarian adult student humor such as “Утре съм на лекции”, “Още едно и тръгвам” and “Стипендията дойде”; essential controls remain straightforward. Bulgarian and English interface settings cover the paytable, feature help, purchase confirmations and history, with proper Cyrillic fonts.

The original VIP God feature follows the supplied five-position/three-opportunity model, not any researched game’s commercial rules. With p = 1 − (1 − 0.048^(1/5))^(1/3) = 0.23075803249924676 per opportunity, full collection succeeds with 4.8% probability and pays 20,000× against a 1,000× stake, giving exactly 96% theoretical return. Incomplete collection pays zero. Show genuine recorded RNG attempts, preserve locked passes, disclose the exact price/chance/zero outcome, and cap the entire paid round at 20,000×. At a €0.20 base bet the debit is €200 and a successful feature awards €4,000.

Original audio will use synthesized city ambience, distant club bass, reel stop cues, a brief stumble effect, separate bonus rhythm layers and VIP lock chimes. Audio starts after interaction and has mute/volume controls. This is an original sound plan; the commercial source recordings will not be reused. Modifier animations are event-driven: stop reels, count original Scatters, reveal frames, resolve Wild motion/multipliers, show splits and ways, present the win using the spin’s current party energy, then show any energy increase or bonus upgrade. Turbo and visual skips affect only presentation.

RTP near 96% for modes other than the supplied God model is a calibration target. Simulations must run through the same engine, divide total settled winnings by actual debits, publish sample size/seed/hit and bonus/max rates, and distinguish measurements from theory. Rare maximum events require an explicit analytic probability plus appropriate tests; a short sample with zero maxima is not evidence of zero probability.
